# Floor 404 — Story and Lore

## Premise
The protagonist is a software engineer working on Floor 4 of a multinational technology company. At the end of a normal workday, he leaves his workroom and discovers that his colleagues have vanished. Their computers, personal belongings, and unfinished work remain.

## Internal design hypothesis — NOT player-confirmed truth
Develop the story with the possibility that the protagonist himself is a simulated conscious person. This is a writing model for consistent clues, **not** an ending to announce as fact. Preserve ambiguity: the player should never receive definitive proof that any encountered layer is base reality.

## Competing interpretations
- A simulated environment malfunctioned and other people disappeared.
- The building is an experiment deliberately testing an observer.
- The day or environment has been reset, perhaps repeatedly.
- Records may be deceptive or misinterpreted; even an apparent escape might not prove anything.

## Story beats
1. Floor 4: mundane end of shift, abrupt absence, first signs of system trouble.
2. Nearby floors: clues about colleagues, access restrictions, infrastructure failures.
3. Later exploration: spatial inconsistencies, contradictory records, observer terminology.
4. Research areas: simulation and consciousness research, but no definitive exposition dump.
5. Exit or finale: emotionally satisfying action, intellectually unresolved reality.

## Motifs
- 404: missing room, missing file, missing identity, missing origin.
- 2003: restrained recurring reference to Nick Bostrom's 2003 simulation argument; use 2–3 purposeful appearances, not a ubiquitous numeric code.
- Distinguish *unavailable information* from proof of nonexistence.

## Philosophical reference
Nick Bostrom, “Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?” (2003). The simulation argument is a philosophical trilemma, not proof that our world is simulated. A bibliography entry can appear as an optional easter egg. Avoid presenting invented quotations as his words.

## Ending policy
Keep exact ending undecided. A seemingly normal exit may contain a subtle inconsistency, but do not force a literal `YOU ARE A SIMULATION` reveal.
